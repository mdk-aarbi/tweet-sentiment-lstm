Tweet Sentiment Classification with LSTMs

Positive, negative or neutral from raw tweet text, trained from scratch and tested with pretrained GloVe vectors.

Muhammad Danial Khalid · BSCS, Superior University, Lahore

The task

Three-class sentiment on tweets

Input

A short tweet, up to 50 tokens after vectorization.

Output

One of three labels: negative, neutral, positive.

Model family

Recurrent network, specifically a bidirectional LSTM.

Dataset

TweetEval sentiment, a public benchmark

45,615

training tweets

2,000

validation tweets

12,284

test tweets

Test labels: 48.33% neutral, 32.34% negative, 19.33% positive. Always predicting neutral already scores 48.33%, so that is the baseline to beat.

Pipeline

From tweet to prediction

1. Vectorize

Keras TextVectorization, 20,000 tokens, length 50.

2. Embed

100 dimensional embedding with masking of padding.

3. Encode

Bidirectional LSTM, 64 units, dropout 0.5.

4. Classify

Dense layer with softmax over three classes.

Architecture

Why a bidirectional LSTM

  • An LSTM reads the tweet in order and keeps context through its gates.
  • Reading both directions lets a word like "not" shape the words after it and before it.
  • The final states of both directions are joined and fed to the classifier.

Experiment

Model A

Embeddings learned from scratch on the training tweets.

Model B

Embeddings initialised from GloVe Twitter 100d and frozen. 87.9% of the vocabulary is covered.

Error analysis

Where the models go wrong

  • The neutral class is the hardest boundary: mild opinions get pulled toward positive or negative.
  • Sarcasm and short context-free tweets are often misread.
  • Confusion matrices and misclassified examples are saved in the repository.
